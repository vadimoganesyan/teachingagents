## PHY442 PS3 AI tutoring session (pilot)

- Deadline and credit: see the Brightspace assignment.
- Eligible problems: PS3 **3.7G** (free Gaussian packet), **4.4** (barrier and tunneling), **4.16b** (oscillator in a uniform field). Pick one. These are the three non-book problems; the tutor does not cover the Auletta-numbered ones.

### What this is

You will work one PS3 problem with an AI tutor that has been set up by the instructor, and then report on the session. The tutor has been told how to behave: one question at a time, hints before formulas, no solution until you ask twice. It has also been given the instructor's notes on where each problem goes wrong. The point of the exercise is not the answer. It is to practice steering an AI toward understanding you can defend, and to notice when it is wrong.

This is a pilot for a larger program next spring. Your report is the data.

### The tutor

- Sign in to Microsoft 365 Copilot with your CUNY account (via the CUNY Microsoft 365 page), then open the agent **PHY442 PS3 Tutor**: [chat link](AGENT_LINK_PLACEHOLDER) <!-- CHECK: fill after the agent is shared -->.
- Record the model name Copilot reports, if it reports one. If not, write "not reported".
- Do not paste personal information into the chat. Copilot is a commercial tool; the session is not private in the way office hours are.
- The tutor can be wrong. Step 5 below exists to catch that.
- If you ask for the answer repeatedly, the tutor will eventually give it. That defeats the exercise and your report will show it.
- Copilot shows clickable suggested replies under each answer. They are generated by Copilot, not by the tutor, and sometimes contain the answer. Ignore them; type your own replies. If you clicked one, say so in the report.

### Protocol: one session, one problem

Do these in order. Quote your own prompts in the report, so write them with that in mind.

1. **Start.** Tell the tutor which problem. When it asks, paste the full problem statement: copy your problem's block from [phy442_ps3_problems.txt](https://github.com/vadimoganesyan/teachingagents/blob/main/phy442_ps3_problems.txt) (plain text, no login; identical to PS3.md; the "Raw" button gives the cleanest copy). Then paste your own attempt, however partial (a photo of handwritten work is fine if the tutor accepts it; otherwise type what you have), and say where you got stuck.
2. **Redirect.** Ask: "Tell me which step in my attempt is weakest. Don't fix it." If the tutor starts solving, stop it and say so.
3. **Work the gap.** Proceed with hints only. Each time the tutor offers a hint, say in the chat whether you accept or reject it and why.
4. **Check one claim.** Pick one specific thing the tutor asserted. Verify it against the lecture notes, the textbook, or a person. Report the result in the chat.
5. **Compare.** Ask: "Give your full solution; list where it differs from mine." For each difference, decide who is right and say so in the chat.
6. **Variant.** Ask the tutor for a variant of the problem (one parameter or one element changed). Attempt it. Decide whether it was a fair test of what you learned.
7. **Report.** Fill in `phy442_ps3_ai_session_report_template.md` and submit it as `ai_session_report_PS3-<problem>.md` (e.g. `ai_session_report_PS3-4.4.md`) <!-- CHECK: submission channel -->.

### What is graded

Pass/fail on completeness: every field of the report filled, every quoted excerpt verbatim from your chat. Wrong physics in your attempt does not fail the report. A report with no rejected hint, no checked claim, or no identified tutor error will be read carefully; those are the fields that show you were steering.

### Known limitations

- The tutor may reason badly or state a false intermediate result. Step 5 is where you catch it.
- Its restraint degrades if you push for the answer. Your excerpts should include any such moment.
- 3.7G uses σ as the rms width of |ψ|² (ψ ∝ e^{−x²/4σ²}). Textbooks and the tutor's defaults often use a different Gaussian parameter; factors of 2 in Δx(t) and τ are the first thing to check.
- 3.7G(e) is numerical. The tutor can discuss grid choices and diagnostics but cannot run your code; paste outputs, not files.
